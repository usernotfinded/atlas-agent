Three of these carry forward from `v0.6.27` unchanged. Each was measured there
and none is blocked on effort, which is the useful thing to know before picking
one up — the full measurements stay in
[v0.6.27 Release Candidates](v0.6.27-candidates.md) rather than being restated.

- **CAND-032** effectively declines itself on its own numbers. Lazy per-domain
  import buys at most 13% of one CLI invocation and at most 4.5% of the suite,
  against threading local imports through a 5.9k-line dispatch whose command
  surface is a pinned trust contract. It is carried forward so the decision is
  recorded rather than forgotten, not because the measurement is expected to
  change.
- **CAND-034** is a vocabulary decision, not a typing exercise. 136 raised codes
  reach the operator as a generic message; eleven different not-found spellings
  already exist across the `-show` commands. Adding that many strings without
  deciding the vocabulary first would make the surface harder to consume.
- **CAND-036** is blocked on an undefined requirement. Three of L3's four
  conditions now hold. The fourth, "active operator oversight", is defined
  nowhere, and a deadman proves the agent is alive rather than that a human is
  watching. Until that has a definition, there is nothing to implement — and no
  amount of implementation would substitute for the external review the
  governance document requires.

### CAND-038 — Three admission rules share the name `validate_provider_id`

`CAND-037` collapsed six helper families in `research/` and stopped at the
seventh. `validate_provider_id` has 21 copies that look like duplicates and are
not. Normalising the error code away leaves three **disjoint** admission rules
under one name:

| Rule | Copies | Admits |
|---|---|---|
| `value in _get_disabled_provider_ids()` | 15 | the four disabled external provider ids, **not** `mock` |
| `value == "mock"` | 5 | `mock` only, **not** the external ids |
| either of the above | 1 | all five |

`mock` is not among the disabled ids, so the first two rules accept strictly
different sets rather than one being a subset of the other.

The union belongs to `provider_mock_response_simulation`, and it is not drift.
That module calls the function on two different fields — its upstream preview's
provider id, which is external, and its own artifact's, which must be `mock` —
and re-checks `provider_id != "mock"` immediately after validating its own. One
member of a family being more permissive than its four siblings is the drift
signature, so this was checked by reading both call sites rather than assumed.

What makes it worth doing is the shape of that defence. A permissive validator
followed by a separate strict check is exactly what breaks when someone deletes
the redundant-looking line. Naming the two policies apart removes the need for
the defence instead of relying on it.

Acceptance would need three named policies, the module needing both saying which
it means at each call site, and **no change to any admitted set** — a change
there would be a policy decision, not a rename, and is out of scope.

This is validator naming only: no gate added or removed, and no change to the
risk gate, kill switch, approval queues, broker adapters, or the audit
hash-chain.

## Safety boundaries

No `v0.6.28` planning content enables live trading, live submit, broker or
provider execution, credential loading, network access, order placement,
pending-order creation, or approval queue mutation. `atlas run --mode live`
remains fail-closed. No candidate here moves a default toward permissiveness.

L4 unattended live autonomy remains out of reach without external legal,
security, operational, risk, broker-adapter and regulatory review by qualified
parties, which
[Bounded Live Autonomy Governance](../bounded-live-autonomy-governance.md)
requires to be done by them and not by self-assessment.
